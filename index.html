Manifold-Constrained MPPI:
Real-Time Sampling-Based Control Under Hard Constraints

Seulchan Lee1, Sanghyun Kim†1,2
1School of Mechanical Engineering, Kyung Hee University, 2Advanced Institute of Convergence Technology (AICT)
Submitted to International Journal of Control, Automation, and Systems (IJCAS), 2026
†Corresponding Author
Paper Code

MC-MPPI is a real-time sampling-based control framework that strictly enforces manifold-based equality constraints, such as the closed-chain kinematic constraints arising in dual-arm manipulation. By decoupling constraint handling into a VAE-learned latent-space planning stage and a KKT-based execution-level projection, MC-MPPI runs at 100 Hz while reducing equality-constraint violation by an order of magnitude over baseline MPPI variants.

Abstract

Sampling-based model predictive control methods such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) offer derivative-free optimization and robustness in complex robotic systems, making them attractive for real-time control. However, standard MPPI relies on cost-based soft penalties that cannot guarantee hard-constraint satisfaction, severely limiting its applicability to constrained tasks such as closed-chain manipulation. To address this, we propose Manifold-Constrained MPPI (MC-MPPI), a real-time sampling-based control framework that handles hard equality constraints while preserving the computational advantages of MPPI. The key idea is to decouple constraint handling into two stages: planning and execution. At the planning level, a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) learns a low-dimensional latent space that captures the structure of the constraint manifold, enabling MPPI to sample candidate trajectories in this latent space and evaluate costs through decoding, without per-sample projection onto the manifold. At the execution level, a KKT-based projection layer enforces constraint satisfaction on the selected trajectory, which is then tracked by an inverse dynamics controller. This decoupled architecture significantly reduces the computational burden of enforcing hard constraints, enabling real-time control of high-dimensional constrained systems. Experiments on multiple constrained robotic systems in both simulation and real-world settings, including closed-chain dual-arm manipulation, demonstrate that MC-MPPI operates at 100 Hz while maintaining constraint satisfaction in real time and significantly outperforming baseline methods in tracking accuracy.

Methodology

Two-Stage Architecture. The upper-level planner runs MPPI in a VAE-learned latent space to efficiently generate near-feasible candidate trajectories under manifold-based equality constraints. The decoded optimal solution is then corrected by a KKT-based projection layer to recover strict feasibility, and the resulting reference is tracked by a lower-level HQP-based inverse-dynamics controller.

Real-world closed-chain dual-arm setup considered in this work. The shared object induces hard kinematic equality constraints between the two manipulators.

Problem Setup

Constraint Manifold and Why Standard MPPI Fails

We consider an n-DoF system whose configuration q is restricted to the equality-constraint manifold M = {q : h(q) = 0}, an (n−l)-dimensional submanifold of zero Lebesgue measure. Naive MPPI sampling in the ambient space therefore yields feasible configurations with probability zero, and the conventional remedy — adding a large penalty cost on ‖h(q)‖ — cannot strictly enforce the constraint and quickly destabilizes closed-chain tasks.

Planning Stage

VAE-Based Latent-Space MPPI

A Variational Autoencoder is pre-trained on feasible configurations on M, and the resulting decoder ψθ : ℝm → ℝn acts as a learned, near-feasible parameterization of the constraint manifold. MC-MPPI runs MPPI directly in this latent space: it propagates a latent state with zt+1 = zt + ũtΔt, decodes each rolled-out latent trajectory back to the joint space via ψθ, and evaluates costs there. A single-instance sampling strategy — using one trajectory-wise noise vector applied uniformly over the prediction horizon — suppresses chattering of decoded motions and yields broader exploration per rollout. Thousands of latent rollouts are evaluated in parallel on the GPU, and the importance-weighted optimum is decoded into a near-feasible reference configuration.

Execution Stage

One-Shot KKT/QP Projection & HQP Tracking

Because ψθ is only an approximation of M, the decoded reference exhibits a residual manifold mismatch. A single-step QP, obtained by linearizing h(q) = 0, projects this reference back onto the manifold while a task-tracking term keeps the projection biased toward the operational-space target. Because the reference is already near-feasible, one QP solve recovers a strictly feasible configuration without iterative refinement, keeping the projection cost negligible. The resulting feasible state is tracked by a Hierarchical Quadratic Programming (HQP)-based inverse-dynamics controller that produces control inputs consistent with the equality constraints of the closed-chain system.

Experiments

We validate MC-MPPI on a closed-chain dual-arm manipulation task in MuJoCo with two Panda manipulators jointly grasping a flat tray (q ∈ ℝ14, equality constraint h(q) ∈ ℝ8 comprising a 6-D relative-pose closure and a 2-D tray-flatness term, latent dimension m = 6). MPPI uses K = 200 samples, horizon T = 30, and Δt = 10 ms on an Intel i5-13400F CPU with an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Ti GPU.

Closed-chain dual-arm manipulation benchmark used throughout the experiments.

Validation of Hard-Constraint Satisfaction

MC-MPPI is compared against Vanilla MPPI (joint-space MPPI with a penalty cost on ‖h(q)‖) and Latent MPPI (latent-space MPPI without QP projection). MC-MPPI reduces the closed-chain violation by nearly an order of magnitude over both baselines, and is the only variant that successfully transports the tray to the target without losing the bimanual grasp.

Validation of Single-Instance Sampling

With single-instance sampling, MC-MPPI converges around static obstacles in 7.74 s, compared to 79.4 s for the conventional step-wise variant, while producing notably smoother joint trajectories. The mean computation time per cycle stays well within the 10 ms budget; without single-instance sampling, peak latency reaches 16.38 ms with a 0.63% deadline-miss rate, whereas the proposed scheme keeps all cycles below the budget.

Reactiveness Under Dynamic Obstacles

Under a single spherical obstacle (radius 5 cm) traversing the workspace at 0.1–0.2 m/s along either the x- or y-axis, MC-MPPI achieves a 100% success rate in three of four conditions while keeping the equality-constraint violation in the 0.0062–0.0070 range, comparable to the static-obstacle case. The only failure mode arises in the x-axis 0.2 m/s setting, where the dual-arm workspace itself bounds the available lateral clearance.

Real-World Deployment

The framework is deployed on a real closed-chain dual-arm system to verify practical applicability under sim-to-real uncertainties such as sensor noise, model mismatch, and unmodeled dynamics. The full hardware experiments and additional ablations are reported in the accompanying IJCAS submission.
